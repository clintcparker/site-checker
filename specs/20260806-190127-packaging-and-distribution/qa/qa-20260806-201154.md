# QA Report: Packaging & Distribution

**QA Mode**: CLI QA

**Verdict**: ⚠️ **PARTIAL PASS**

## QA Summary

Forty scenarios were derived from `spec.md`'s six user stories, its twenty-seven functional
requirements, and `quickstart.md`'s eight numbered validation scenarios. Thirty of them can be
executed without publishing anything; **all thirty ran, and none failed** — twenty-four clean
passes and six partials, every partial being a latent hardening gap already named by the staff
review rather than a broken acceptance criterion. The ten that did not run are blocked on
something outside this branch: two maintainer decisions (a public repository, a $99/yr Apple
Developer membership), the seven credentials those decisions gate, a published `v1.0.0`, and one
broken tool on this machine.

Testing was deliberately behavioural rather than static wherever the shape allowed it. This
feature's logic lives in ~400 lines of workflow YAML, so the inline shell of every guard —
`ci.yml`'s version sentinel, `release.yml`'s tag validation, its one-time-setup probe, its
two-direction render guard, and its tap-commit skip — was lifted verbatim and executed under
`/bin/bash` against the real template and the real repository state, with deliberately broken
copies supplying the negative cases. Two full `pnpm tauri build` runs were performed: one with
`Cargo.toml` stamped to `9.9.9` and no `--bundles` flag, which proved both that the reduced
default targets cannot reach the DMG bundler and that deleting `tauri.conf.json`'s `version` key
genuinely makes `Cargo.toml` authoritative (`CFBundleShortVersionString` → `9.9.9`); and one with
`--bundles app` on the restored sentinel. Three steps of `verify-install-channels.yml` were run
live against the real GitHub API and the real tap, where each correctly failed closed. The
tap-commit step was exercised against a throwaway local tap through all three of its states —
first release, byte-identical re-run, and the post-rebuild checksum change that research R14 says
is the common case — producing exactly one `Casks/` file and exactly two commits.

Three things are worth stating plainly rather than leaving to the tables. First, **no scenario
here was skipped for want of GUI automation.** This feature's `screenshots/` directory holds a
`SKIPPED.md`, not a `manifest.json`, because the screenshots extension judged at `before` time
that the feature changes no UI surface — a judgement this QA independently re-checked and
confirmed (`app.windows` in `tauri.conf.json` is untouched; zero lines changed under `src/` or
`src-tauri/src/`). The app is nonetheless demonstrably drivable in this environment: the previous
feature's manifest records `capture_blocked: false` for both its `before` and `after` passes. The
two acceptance steps here that would involve driving the app — opening the installed copy to
confirm no security dialog (SC-002), and adding a site before testing the two uninstall paths
(SC-010) — are blocked because **there is no published, notarized, cask-installed copy to drive**,
not because driving is impossible. Second, the one QA action deliberately *not* taken: `brew audit
--cask --new` (T017's open half) still cannot run, because `brew config` on this machine aborts
loading its vendored `json` gem. The repair command is recorded in `tasks.md`, but it is an
`rm -rf` against the user's Homebrew installation outside this repository, and running it
unattended is not QA's call. Third, this QA touched the user's data not at all: the app was built
twice but never launched, and `~/Library/Application Support/com.clintparker.site-checker/` was
never read or written. `src-tauri/Cargo.toml` and `Cargo.lock` were temporarily stamped and are
restored — the worktree is clean apart from the untracked `qa/` and `reviews/` directories.

The verdict is PARTIAL PASS rather than ALL PASSED because ten acceptance scenarios remain
unexecuted and six runnable ones surfaced real gaps. It is not FAILURES FOUND: nothing that could
be tested, failed.

### TC-027: Daily verification is blind to checksum drift (review R001)

**Status**: ⚠️ PARTIAL — latent gap, highest-value item on this list
**Steps to Reproduce**: `grep -i -E 'sha256|shasum|checksum|digest' .github/workflows/verify-install-channels.yml` → no matches. Read step 2: it is `curl -sfIL`, a HEAD request.
**Expected**: US4 exists to catch "green release, broken install".
**Actual**: The job proves an asset URL *resolves* and that the cask's `version` string matches, but never compares the cask's two `sha256` values against the bytes attached to the release. Research R14 establishes that a re-run uploads **different** bytes for the same tag, so a run where `release` succeeds and `homebrew` fails — an expired `TAP_PUSH_TOKEN` is the documented example, and `docs/how-to/release.md:113` says such a token passes pre-flight — leaves a tap whose version and URL are both correct and whose checksums are stale. Steps 2 and 3 pass, `e2e` is off the cron, and verification is green while `brew install` aborts on a SHA256 mismatch.
**Evidence**: [responses/tc-026-033-findings.txt](responses/tc-026-033-findings.txt)
**Severity**: Medium — it degrades the detection this feature's US4 exists to provide, in the direction that breaks users rather than the maintainer. FR-020's *literal* text is met, which is why this is a partial rather than a failure.

### TC-023: The post-release tap check can race a CDN cache (review R002)

**Status**: ⚠️ PARTIAL
**Steps to Reproduce**: `curl -sSI https://raw.githubusercontent.com/clintcparker/homebrew-tap/main/Formula/name-on.rb`
**Expected**: Research R12 dropped `name-on`'s retry loop on the stated ground that raw content "is available essentially immediately".
**Actual**: Measured live during this QA — `cache-control: max-age=300`, `via: 1.1 varnish`, `x-served-by: cache-sjc10051-SJC`. `workflow_run` fires seconds after `homebrew` pushes, so step 3 can read a cask up to ~5 minutes stale and report "the tap is lagging the latest release" against a perfectly good release. Exposure is bounded — it needs a prior request to have primed the same POP, which the daily cron does once a day.
**Evidence**: [responses/tc-026-033-findings.txt](responses/tc-026-033-findings.txt)
**Severity**: Medium — a false red on a good release, and the premise in research R12 is now measurably wrong and should be corrected in place so the next person does not repeat the reasoning.

### TC-028: `workflow_run` has no conclusion filter (review R003)

**Status**: ⚠️ PARTIAL
**Steps to Reproduce**: Read `verify-install-channels.yml:11-13` and `:84`.
**Expected**: The `macos-latest` `e2e` job is the most expensive step in the feature and is deliberately kept off the daily cron for that reason.
**Actual**: `types: [completed]` fires on failure as well as success, and `e2e`'s only guard is `github.event_name != 'schedule'`. **Evidence**: [responses/tc-026-033-findings.txt](responses/tc-026-033-findings.txt)
**Severity**: Low-Medium — cost and noise, not correctness.
